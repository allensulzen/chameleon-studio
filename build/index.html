Perfboard build

Everything the breadboard left out: the 9 V power entry, the TL072 input buffer, the 5×7 cm board that carries the Seed, and the enclosure. Same footswitch, pots and jacks you already wired — they move over unchanged.

Power, plainly

The Seed wants 3.3 V inside, and it makes that itself from whatever you feed it. It accepts either of two sources, and it's safe to have both plugged in at once:

  • USB-C gives it 5 V. That's how it's been running so far, and it's how you flash it. It powers the Seed only — nothing else on the board.
  • VIN (pin 39) takes 4–17 V, so a standard 9 V pedalboard supply goes straight in. This is how the finished pedal runs. The 9 V rail also feeds the TL072, which needs more than 3.3 V to work.

So: on stage, 9 V only. On the bench, USB for flashing plus 9 V if you want to hear the buffered input. USB alone still makes sound, but the guitar is going through an unpowered buffer — that's why the board below includes a bypass jumper.

Pedal supplies are centre-negative: the outer barrel of the plug is +9 V and the centre pin is ground, the opposite of most wall warts. The 1N5817 diode makes a backwards plug harmless (nothing happens instead of something dying), and the 470 µF capacitor smooths out supply noise so it doesn't end up as hum.

Your pots' three wires

Red and white are the two outer lugs, yellow is the wiper (middle). Yes — all three reds meet at one point (3V3 analog, Seed pin 21) and all three whites meet at another (AGND, pin 20). Each yellow goes to its own pin: 22, 23, 24. Every pot is the same voltage divider between the same two rails; only the wiper is unique. If a knob turns backwards, swap that pot's red and white.

Schematic

Read it left to right: the DC jack feeds the 9 V rail, which powers the Seed (VIN) and the op-amp. The guitar goes through the buffer into Seed pin 16, and the Seed's pin 18 goes out through 100 Ω to the amp jack. Pots and footswitch are unchanged from the breadboard.

Why the buffer needs 9 V. A TL072 won't run on the Seed's 3.3 V — it needs at least ~7 V across it. So the finished pedal runs on the 9 V supply; USB alone still runs the Seed (and flashes it) but leaves the op-amp asleep and the guitar path silent. JP1 exists for exactly that case on the bench. Both supplies plugged in at once is fine.

Board layout — 5×7 cm perfboard (18 × 24 holes)

Enclosure

A 1590B-size box (or the 3D print from the plan) takes the 5×7 board flat on the floor with the Seed's USB socket facing a hole in the end wall, so you can flash it without opening the box. Drill: pots 7 mm, ¼″ jacks 10 mm, footswitch 12 mm, DC jack 12 mm (check your jack — many 2.1 mm panel jacks want 8 mm), USB-C access slot 10 × 5 mm, LED window 3 mm above the Seed's LED if you want to see it.

Line the inside with copper foil tape, soldering every overlap so the foil is one sheet electrically, and run one wire from the foil to the input jack's sleeve lug. That single wire is what makes the 480 MHz chip inaudible through a high-gain amp. Do not ground the foil anywhere else.

Build order, with a check after each step

  1. Headers first, nothing else

    Solder the two 20-pin female headers into columns 1 and 7, rows 1–20, using the Seed itself (unplugged, unpowered) as the jig so they're parallel. Remove the Seed before doing anything else.

    Check The Seed slides in and out without forcing. USB socket points where you planned the slot.

  2. Power entry

    D3 (band toward the Seed), C6 (long leg to 9 V), the 9 V and GND bus wires along row 24, wire-in pads for the DC jack. Wire the DC jack: its barrel/sleeve lug to the D3 anode pad, its centre-pin lug to GND.

    Check With the Seed OUT, plug in a 9 V pedal supply and measure ≈ 8.7 V between the C6 legs (9 V minus the diode). Plug the supply in backwards on purpose: 0 V, nothing warm. Then Seed IN, USB unplugged, 9 V in: user LED comes on solid. Measure 3.3 V on pin 21.

  3. Buffer

    U1 socket, then R2/R3/C4 for the 4.5 V reference, C5 on the supply pin, R1/C1 on the input, R4∥C3 feedback, C2/R5 out, D1/D2 at the Seed-side node, JP1 header. Tie the spare half off (pin 5 → 4.5 V, pin 6 → pin 7). Chip in last, notch matching the socket.

    Check 9 V in, no Seed: pin 8 of U1 reads ≈ 8.7 V, pin 4 reads 0, pin 3 and pin 1 both read ≈ 4.35 V (half of 8.7). If pin 1 is pinned at 0 or 8.7 V the feedback wire is missing. Then Seed in, guitar into J1, amp on J2 at low volume: clean sound on patch C with the top end back — compare to JP1 jumpered.

  4. Output, controls, switch

    R6 from pin 18 to the OUT wire-in pad. Pot reds to the 3V3A pad (pin 21), whites to the AGND pad (pin 20), yellows to the pads for 22/23/24. Footswitch pads: pin 28 and GND (pin 40).

    Check Exactly what the breadboard did: tap = LED off/on, hold = blinks the patch number, pots move Gain/Tone/Level on patch A.

  5. Box it

    Drill, foil, mount the panel parts, drop the board in, connect the foil to the input jack sleeve, close it up.

    Check High-gain amp, gain up, nothing playing: no whine. Rock the pedal on the floor: no crackle from the jacks. Flash it through the USB slot from the studio without opening the box.
